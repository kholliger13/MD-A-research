# Project 1 — Edition A: Costco Wholesale Corporation (COST)

**Status:** Initial investment-analysis draft for educational use; not personal investment advice.

## Decision, user, company, and valuation date

**Decision for the fund investment committee:** whether to initiate a position in Costco Wholesale Corporation (NASDAQ: COST).  
**Company:** Costco Wholesale Corporation.  
**Valuation date:** October 8, 2025, the filing date of the selected fiscal 2025 Form 10-K. This date fixes the initial information set for this draft; it is not a current market valuation.  
**Initial thesis:** **Watch / defer.** Costco's operating record appears strong, but a recommendation to initiate requires a market price and a valuation relative to intrinsic value—neither is established by the 10-K alone.

## Known evidence from the Form 10-K

All financial-statement amounts below are in **U.S. dollars and millions**, unless stated otherwise.

- Costco's fiscal year ended August 31, 2025. Its Form 10-K was filed October 8, 2025 (SEC accession no. 0000909832-25-000101).
- FY2025 net sales were **$269,912 million**, up 8% from FY2024. Total-company comparable sales increased **6%**. Management identifies increasing net sales, particularly comparable sales, as the most important driver of profitability.
- FY2025 membership-fee revenue was **$5,323 million**, up 10%. Membership renewal rates were 92.3% in the U.S. and Canada and 89.8% worldwide at year-end.
- FY2025 net income was **$8,099 million**; diluted EPS was **$18.21**, based on **444.803 million** diluted weighted-average shares.
- At August 31, 2025, cash and cash equivalents were **$14,161 million** and short-term investments were **$1,123 million**.
- Total long-term debt was **$5,805 million** (including current portion); short-term borrowings outstanding were immaterial.
- Costco operated **914 warehouses** worldwide at August 31, 2025, versus 890 at September 1, 2024.

## Consequential assumptions

- Comparable-sales growth and membership renewals are useful forward indicators of sales and cash-generation durability.
- The fiscal 2025 year-end balance-sheet position is representative enough to form a starting view of capital structure as of the valuation date.
- The 10-K's reported results are an appropriate base year, while later quarterly results must update the view before an investment decision.
- A premium valuation may be warranted only if Costco can sustain high renewal rates, comparable-sales growth, and disciplined expansion without meaningful margin deterioration.

## Unknowns that could change the decision

- COST's closing share price, equity value, and enterprise value on October 8, 2025.
- The valuation implied by comparable companies and by a discounted-cash-flow model.
- Post-year-end operating trends, including comparable sales, membership growth, e-commerce, gasoline-price effects, foreign-exchange effects, and new-warehouse performance.
- The durability of gross-margin improvement, competitive pressures, and the impact of future membership-fee changes.
- Whether current market expectations already price in the operating strength observed in FY2025.

## Research plan

1. Obtain the October 8, 2025 closing share price and calculate equity value using the appropriate diluted share count; calculate enterprise value using debt, cash, and investments consistently.
2. Update the financial base with the next available 10-Q and earnings materials, separating recurring operating changes from gasoline-price and foreign-exchange effects.
3. Build a simple historical model for revenue, comparable sales, membership fees, gross margin, SG&A, operating income, capital expenditures, and free cash flow.
4. Value Costco using a DCF with explicit revenue, margin, reinvestment, and discount-rate assumptions; compare the result with EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, and P/E multiples for relevant large-format retailers.
5. Run downside cases for lower renewal rates, slower comparable-sales growth, margin compression, and more capital-intensive warehouse expansion.
6. State an initiate, watch/defer, or do-not-initiate recommendation only after comparing estimated intrinsic value with the valuation-date market price and documenting the margin of safety.
