# Costco Lab 08 — Deal Evidence and Valuation Triangulation

## Research Question

What would one share of Costco Wholesale Corporation be worth at defensible peer P/E multiples, and how does that valuation compare with my Week 3 discounted cash flow valuation?

**Valuation date:** September 9, 2026, the common closing-price date in my saved Week 3 DCF. The [`costco_dcf_inputs.md`](costco_dcf_inputs.md) record identifies the dated Costco close. I use only earnings published by that date.

## Target Company

Company: Costco Wholesale Corporation (NASDAQ: COST)

Costco operates membership-based warehouse clubs that sell a limited selection of merchandise at low prices and high sales volumes. Costco earns revenue primarily from merchandise sales and membership fees. A useful peer should therefore have meaningful similarities in large-scale retail, recurring customer traffic, low-price/high-volume merchandising, membership economics, or warehouse-club operations.

## Peer Selection Policy

I will consider publicly traded operating companies that have meaningful similarities to Costco's retail economics. Strong candidates should have large-scale consumer retail operations, significant grocery/general merchandise exposure, high inventory turnover, price-focused merchandising, and/or a membership-based retail model.

I will qualify companies that share important retail economics with Costco but have meaningful differences in business model, geographic exposure, membership structure, product mix, or scale.

I will exclude companies whose primary business economics differ substantially from Costco, even if they are commonly classified as retailers.

For valuation, I will use annual reported diluted EPS that was publicly available by my Week 3 valuation date and stock prices from the same trading date for Costco and each peer.

## Peer Candidates — Initial Hypotheses

I will investigate BJ's Wholesale Club (BJ) and Walmart (WMT). These are initial views, not final peer decisions. I will apply the peer selection policy before using either company's P/E multiple.

### BJ's Wholesale Club

**Initial view: Likely USE.**

BJ's operates a membership-based warehouse-club model that is economically similar to Costco. Both companies use membership programs and warehouse-format retailing to sell groceries and general merchandise. BJ's [FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1531152/000153115226000007/bj-20260131.htm) describes its membership clubs and merchandise mix.

**Potential limitation:** BJ's is substantially smaller than Costco and has less geographic diversification.

### Walmart

**Initial view: QUALIFY.**

Walmart shares several important economics with Costco, including large-scale grocery and general merchandise retailing, low-price positioning, high sales volumes, and significant purchasing scale. Walmart's [FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) reports $483.0 billion of Walmart U.S. net sales, illustrating the scale of its retail operations.

**Qualification:** Walmart primarily operates traditional retail and e-commerce businesses rather than Costco's membership warehouse-club model. It is useful as a broader retail comparison but is less directly comparable than BJ's.

Walmart does own Sam's Club, a membership warehouse segment. Its consolidated FY2026 [10-K Business section](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm) identifies Walmart U.S., Walmart International, and Sam's Club U.S. as three segments. Sam's Club U.S. reported approximately $93.0 billion of net sales versus $706.4 billion for Walmart overall, so Walmart's consolidated EPS reflects much more than warehouse-club operations.

I will make the final peer decisions based on business comparability, regardless of whether a company's valuation multiple supports the DCF result.

## Peer P/E Multiples

P/E = September 9, 2026 closing price ÷ latest publicly available annual reported diluted EPS.

| Peer | Calculation | P/E |
|---|---:|---:|
| BJ's Wholesale Club | $88.51 ÷ $4.38 | **20.21×** |
| Walmart | $105.83 ÷ $2.73 | **38.77×** |

Calculations use unrounded input prices and reported EPS; only the displayed P/E is rounded to two decimals. Costco is the valuation target, not part of the peer multiple set.

## Costco Share Price Implied by Peer P/E

Implied Costco price = peer P/E × Costco FY2025 reported diluted EPS of **$18.21**. I calculate with each peer's unrounded P/E, then round the share price to cents.

| Peer multiple applied to Costco | Calculation using unrounded peer P/E | Implied Costco price |
|---|---:|---:|
| BJ's (20.21× displayed) | ($88.51 ÷ $4.38) × $18.21 | **$367.98** |
| Walmart (38.77× displayed; qualified peer) | ($105.83 ÷ $2.73) × $18.21 | **$705.92** |

This gives a peer-implied range of **$367.98–$705.92 per Costco share**. Each figure is a conditional estimate: it asks what Costco's annual earnings would be worth at that peer's observed P/E, not what Costco stock actually traded for. Walmart's business-model differences make its estimate a broader comparison.

## Conclusion

I used BJ's Wholesale Club as a direct peer because its membership-based warehouse model most closely resembles Costco's business economics. I qualified Walmart as a broader comparison because it shares Costco's large-scale, low-price grocery and general merchandise economics but does not rely primarily on the membership warehouse model.

The peer P/E analysis implies a Costco value of approximately **$367.98 to $705.92 per share**, compared with my Week 3 DCF **base estimate of $440.22 per share**. The DCF's saved sensitivity grid spans **$259.15 to $425.92 per share** under 9%–11% WACC and 2%–4% terminal-growth scenarios; it is separate from the 8% WACC base case.

The DCF base estimate lies **inside** the peer P/E range: $72.24 above the BJ's-based price and $265.70 below the Walmart-based price. The September 9 closing price of $902.60 exceeds both peer-implied prices and the DCF base estimate.

The wide peer P/E range is primarily driven by BJ's **20.21×** multiple versus Walmart's **38.77×** multiple. Differences between those market-based estimates and the DCF also reflect the DCF's placeholder cash-flow growth, discount-rate, and terminal-growth assumptions. I do not mechanically average the methods because the DCF values forecast cash flows while peer P/E applies observed market multiples to reported earnings, and their assumptions differ.

My current call remains **watch/defer**. I would reconsider this conclusion if a sourced Costco-specific cost of capital and normalized cash-flow forecast supported a materially different value, evidence justified a sustained Costco premium to these peers, or the share price fell below a valuation supported by those inputs.
